What is Jacobson’s Progressive Muscle Relaxation (JPMR)?

Jacobson’s Progressive Muscle Relaxation (JPMR) is a simple relaxation technique developed by Dr. Edmund Jacobson. It helps you reduce stress by teaching you to recognize and release muscle tension in different parts of your body.

The basic idea is simple: first, gently tighten a group of muscles for a few seconds. Then, slowly relax those muscles and notice the difference between tension and relaxation.

Our body and mind are closely connected. When we are anxious, stressed, worried, or angry, our muscles become tense. Similarly, when the muscles relax, the mind also becomes calmer.

Regular practice of JPMR can help improve both physical and mental well-being.

Before you start

  • Choose a time when you are unlikely to be disturbed.
  • Find a comfortable place and sit in a chair or lie on a bed.
  • Wear loose, comfortable clothing and remove tight belts, watches, or shoes if possible.
  • Keep your phone on silent mode.
  • Dim the lights, keep the room quiet, and ensure the temperature is comfortable.
  • Do not practice immediately after a heavy meal.
  • Avoid alcohol or stimulants beforehand.
  • Never tighten muscles so much that it causes pain.
  • If you have an injury, recent surgery, or severe arthritis, avoid tightening the affected muscles.

Step-by-step practice

01

Deep breathing

Before starting

Take three slow deep breaths. Breathe in through your nose for four seconds, hold for two seconds, and breathe out slowly through your mouth for six seconds. Repeat three to five times.

02

Hands and forearms

Tighten

Make a tight fist with both hands and squeeze gently. Hold for 5–10 seconds and notice the tension in your fingers, palms, and forearms.

Relax

Open your hands slowly, let your fingers loosen completely, and rest for 15–20 seconds. Pay attention to the feeling of warmth and relaxation.

03

Upper arms

Tighten

Bend your elbows and tighten your upper arms. Hold for 5–10 seconds.

Relax

Let your arms drop comfortably and feel the tension leaving your muscles. Relax for 15–20 seconds.

04

Forehead

Tighten

Raise your eyebrows as high as possible and hold for 5 seconds.

Relax

Let your forehead become smooth and relaxed and notice the difference.

05

Eyes and cheeks

Tighten

Close your eyes tightly and hold for 5 seconds.

Relax

Open your eyes slowly and let the muscles around your eyes relax.

06

Jaw and mouth

Tighten

Clench your teeth gently and press your lips together. Hold for 5 seconds.

Relax

Allow your jaw to drop slightly and relax your mouth completely. Notice how much tension was present.

07

Neck and shoulders

Tighten

Lift your shoulders toward your ears and hold for 5–10 seconds.

Relax

Let your shoulders drop naturally. Many people carry stress in this region, so spend extra time relaxing your shoulders.

08

Chest

Tighten

Take a deep breath and hold it for 5 seconds.

Relax

Slowly exhale and feel your chest relax. Take normal breaths again.

09

Abdomen

Tighten

Pull your stomach inward or tighten your abdominal muscles. Hold for 5–10 seconds.

Relax

Release the tension and let your stomach soften.

10

Lower back and hips

Tighten

Press your lower back gently against the chair or bed and hold for 5 seconds.

Relax

Release slowly and notice how the lower back feels lighter.

11

Thighs

Tighten

Press your thighs together and hold for 5–10 seconds.

Relax

Let your thighs become loose and comfortable.

12

Calves

Tighten

Point your toes upward toward your head and hold for 5–10 seconds.

Relax

Let your feet relax completely.

13

Feet and toes

Tighten

Curl your toes downward and hold for 5 seconds.

Relax

Stretch your toes comfortably and notice the relaxation spreading through your feet.

14

Whole-body relaxation

Close your eyes and focus on your entire body. Notice how relaxed each muscle group feels. Take three slow breaths and imagine that with each breath out, stress is leaving your body.

Remain relaxed for two or three minutes. When you are ready, wiggle your fingers and toes, stretch gently, and open your eyes slowly.

How long does JPMR take?

15–20 minutes Full session
5–10 minutes Short version
1–2 times Daily practice
Expected timeline
The best results usually appear after 2–4 weeks of regular practice.

Tips for better results

  • Practice at the same time every day.
  • Find a quiet place where you can focus.
  • Focus on the difference between tension and relaxation.
  • Combine JPMR with slow breathing.
  • Be patient; relaxation improves with regular practice.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Tightening muscles too hard.
  • Holding your breath.
  • Practicing immediately after a heavy meal.
  • Expecting instant results.
  • Practicing irregularly.

Safety precautions

  • Consult your doctor before practicing if you have severe heart disease, recent surgery, severe muscle injury, severe arthritis, or uncontrolled high blood pressure.
  • Stop the exercise if you experience severe pain, dizziness, shortness of breath, or chest pain.

Frequently asked questions

Can I do JPMR while lying in bed?

Yes. Many people practice it before sleep to improve sleep quality.

Will it replace my medicines?

No. JPMR is an additional technique and should not replace medicines prescribed by your doctor.

How soon will I feel better?

Some people feel relaxed after the first session, but regular practice for several weeks gives the best results.

Can children and older adults practice JPMR?

Yes, with appropriate guidance.

Final message

Stress causes muscles to tighten, and tense muscles send signals of stress back to the brain. JPMR breaks this cycle by teaching your body how to relax.

Just 15 minutes of practice every day can help you sleep better, reduce anxiety, and improve your overall sense of well-being.
