What is sleep hygiene?

Sleep hygiene refers to the daily habits and environmental factors that help you get good-quality sleep. Just as brushing your teeth helps maintain oral health, healthy sleep habits help maintain physical and mental well-being.

Poor sleep hygiene can lead to difficulty falling asleep, frequent awakenings, daytime tiredness, poor concentration, irritability, and anxiety or depression.

Good sleep hygiene is not a medicine—it is a set of simple lifestyle practices that can improve your sleep naturally.

Recommended sleep duration
Most adults need 7–9 hours of sleep every night.

Signs of poor sleep hygiene

  • Take more than 30 minutes to fall asleep.
  • Wake up repeatedly during the night.
  • Feel tired despite sleeping for many hours.
  • Feel sleepy during the day.
  • Depend on sleeping pills.
  • Sleep at different times every day.
  • Use your mobile phone late at night.

12 practical habits to improve sleep

01

Maintain a regular sleep schedule

Go to bed at the same time every night and wake up at the same time every morning. Maintain the schedule even on weekends.

Remember: Even after a poor night’s sleep, wake up at your usual time.

02

Avoid long daytime naps

Long naps can interfere with nighttime sleep. Limit naps to 20–30 minutes and avoid naps after 3 PM.

03

Create a relaxing bedtime routine

Spend 30–60 minutes winding down before bed with reading, meditation, deep breathing, warm bath, or relaxation exercises such as JPMR.

Avoid stressful discussions, office work, social media, and exciting television programs.

04

Limit screen time

The blue light from phones, tablets, laptops, and televisions suppresses melatonin, the hormone that regulates sleep. Stop using screens at least one hour before bedtime and keep your phone away from your bed.

05

Avoid caffeine in the evening

Caffeine is a stimulant that can remain in the body for several hours. Avoid caffeine after 4 PM.

06

Avoid smoking and nicotine

Nicotine stimulates the brain and can make it difficult to fall asleep. Avoid smoking for several hours before bedtime.

07

Avoid alcohol as a sleep aid

Although alcohol may make you sleepy initially, it often causes frequent awakenings, poor sleep quality, early morning awakening, and restless sleep. Alcohol is not a treatment for insomnia.

08

Exercise regularly

Regular exercise improves sleep quality, mood, energy levels, and stress management. Morning, afternoon, and early evening exercise are often best. Avoid vigorous exercise within 2–3 hours of bedtime.

09

Avoid heavy meals before bed

Heavy meals can cause acidity, indigestion, discomfort, and poor sleep. Eat dinner 2–3 hours before bedtime and avoid spicy or oily foods late at night.

10

Limit fluids before bedtime

Drinking large amounts of fluids at night may cause repeated awakenings to use the bathroom. Reduce fluid intake 1–2 hours before sleep.

11

Create a sleep-friendly bedroom

Keep your bedroom quiet, dark, comfortable, clean, and cool. Use curtains, eye masks, earplugs, and comfortable pillows if needed.

12

Use your bed only for sleep

Your brain should associate the bed with sleep, not work or entertainment. Avoid watching television, eating, working, or scrolling on your phone in bed.

What if you cannot sleep?

  • If you cannot sleep after 20 minutes, get out of bed.
  • Read a book or listen to calming music.
  • Return to bed only when you feel sleepy.
  • Avoid forcing yourself to sleep.
  • Do not watch the clock; constantly checking the time increases stress.

Common myths about sleep

Myth: “Alcohol helps me sleep.”

Reality: Alcohol causes poor-quality sleep.

Myth: “I can catch up on sleep during weekends.”

Reality: Oversleeping disrupts your biological clock.

Myth: “Watching television helps me sleep.”

Reality: Screen exposure delays sleep.

Myth: “Staying in bed longer helps insomnia.”

Reality: Spending too much time in bed can worsen insomnia.

When should you consult a doctor?

  • Sleep problems persist for more than three months.
  • You snore loudly or stop breathing during sleep.
  • You feel excessively sleepy during the day.
  • Sleep problems affect your work or relationships.
  • You depend on sleeping pills regularly.
  • You have symptoms of anxiety or depression.

Key take-home messages

  • Follow a regular sleep schedule.
  • Reduce screen time before bed.
  • Avoid caffeine, nicotine, and alcohol at night.
  • Exercise regularly.
  • Create a relaxing bedtime routine.
  • Keep your bedroom comfortable.
  • Practice relaxation exercises.

Final message

Healthy sleep is one of the pillars of good physical and mental health. Small lifestyle changes practiced consistently can lead to better sleep, improved mood, and a healthier life.
