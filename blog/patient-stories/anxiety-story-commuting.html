Anxiety and commuting

Finding a Calmer Way to Approach the Daily Commute

An illustrative first-person story about travel-related worry, its effect on routine and asking for professional support.

Illustrative composite scenario · Not a real patient testimonial

My commute used to be routine. Then I began worrying about delays, missing a connection or feeling unwell on the way. I checked travel updates again and again and started leaving much earlier than I needed to. Even at home, I was already thinking about the next journey.

When I noticed I was turning down plans because of the trip involved, I decided to speak with a qualified professional. I described what I feared and how travel anxiety was affecting work and time with friends.

Understanding the pattern

We talked about what situations triggered the worry, how I responded and whether there were practical issues that needed planning. My professional did not assume every concern was irrational; we considered what was a real travel problem and what was anxiety making harder.

I chose one small, sensible change: checking the travel information once before leaving rather than repeatedly refreshing it. I discussed how this felt at follow-up and adjusted it when circumstances changed. It was an experiment, not a rule for every trip.

Making plans that leave room for life

I am learning that I can acknowledge a worry without letting it make every decision for me. I still plan for ordinary delays, but I am also talking with my professional about the anxiety and its effect on my routine.

What this story can remind us: Anxiety can start to shape even familiar routines. A professional can help explore what is happening and what support may be appropriate.

When should you seek support?

If worry about commuting or travel is persistent, distressing or affecting daily life, consider seeking professional advice. This story is not a reason to disregard genuine travel or health risks.
