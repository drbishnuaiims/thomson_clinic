Driving anxiety

Getting Back Behind the Wheel at Your Own Pace

An illustrative first-person story about driving-related worry, daily routines and asking for safe, appropriate support.

Illustrative composite scenario · Not a real patient testimonial

After one stressful drive in heavy traffic, I started feeling tense whenever I thought about getting in the car. I began taking longer routes to avoid busy roads and asked other people to drive when possible. At first it seemed like a practical choice, but soon I was worrying about even familiar journeys.

I noticed the anxiety was narrowing my options. I talked with a qualified healthcare professional about the fear and how it was affecting my daily life. We also considered whether there were practical driving-safety concerns that needed separate attention.

Understanding what feels unsafe

I described what I feared might happen and when the worry felt strongest. My professional helped me consider what support could be appropriate. I was not told to force myself onto the road or to ignore safety rules.

We discussed options based on my circumstances. I chose to learn more about support before deciding what to try, and I kept following road-safety guidance. I wanted any step to be appropriate rather than a challenge I had to prove myself against.

Moving forward without pressure

I am learning that anxiety can be addressed without shame, and that progress does not mean taking unsafe risks. The right pace and support may look different for each person.

What this story can remind us: Avoidance can grow when a fear begins to shape daily routines. Ask for help, and keep practical safety at the centre of decisions.

When to ask for help

If driving-related anxiety is restricting everyday life or causing significant distress, consider speaking with a qualified professional. Do not put yourself or others at risk to test a fear.
