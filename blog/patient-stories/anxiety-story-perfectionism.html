Perfectionism and anxiety

When “Good Enough” Never Feels Like Enough

An illustrative first-person story about fear of mistakes, repeated checking and asking for support.

Illustrative composite scenario · Not a real patient testimonial

I wanted every task to be just right. I checked emails several times before sending them and delayed starting projects because I was afraid of getting them wrong. People often called me thorough, but I felt tense and exhausted by the effort.

When I realised that my standards were affecting sleep and making it harder to finish work, I spoke with a qualified professional. I explained how much time checking took and how anxious I felt when I tried to stop.

Understanding what sits behind the high standards

We explored how I responded to mistakes and what I feared they would mean. My professional helped me consider whether anxiety was part of the picture and what support could fit my needs. I was not told to be careless or to abandon work that mattered.

I chose a small task to discuss at follow-up: sending one routine message after a single planned review. I approached it at my own pace and talked about what came up. It was not a test of whether I could handle every situation perfectly.

Learning without judging myself

I am beginning to see that a mistake, if one happens, does not define my whole ability. I still care about doing good work, but I am learning to notice when worry is taking over. Support is a process, and what is useful can vary between people.

What this story can remind us: It is possible to value careful work and still seek help when perfectionism and worry become exhausting.

When to seek support

If fear of mistakes or repeated checking is causing distress or affecting daily functioning, consider speaking with a qualified healthcare professional.
