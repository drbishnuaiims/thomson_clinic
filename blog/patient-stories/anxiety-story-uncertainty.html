Anxiety and uncertainty

Making Decisions When You Cannot Know Every Outcome

An illustrative first-person story about overthinking everyday choices and finding support for persistent worry.

Illustrative composite scenario · Not a real patient testimonial

I could spend an entire evening deciding whether to accept an invitation or send a message. I imagined every possible outcome, asked friends what they thought and still felt unsure. Even small choices started to feel like tests I could get wrong.

I did not call it anxiety at first. I thought I was being careful. But the constant thinking left me tired and made it harder to enjoy plans once I had made them. I decided to talk to a qualified professional about the pattern.

Noticing when thinking stops helping

I described how long I had been worrying and how it affected my days. We talked about stress, health and what I wanted to be able to do again. I learned that a professional could help me assess the concern rather than reducing it to a personality trait.

With support, I began separating decisions that needed more information from decisions that could be made with what I already knew. I tried this gently and discussed how it felt at follow-up. It was not a rule to rush or ignore real risks.

Learning to leave room for not knowing

I am learning that I cannot guarantee every outcome before taking a reasonable next step. My professional and I continue to review what support suits me. Some days I still overthink, but I am less likely to treat that as proof I have failed.

What this story can remind us: Overthinking may deserve attention when it takes up significant time or limits everyday choices. You can ask for help without self-diagnosing.

When to seek support for anxiety

If worry feels difficult to manage or affects sleep, work, study or relationships, consider speaking with a qualified healthcare professional.
