Adjusting to a Major Change While Feeling Low

An illustrative story about a difficult transition, persistent low mood and recognising when to ask for help.

Illustrative composite scenario · Not a real patient testimonial

After a major change in my life, I expected an adjustment period. But the weeks went by and I stayed low, stopped enjoying my usual routines and found it harder to make simple plans. I kept telling myself I should be grateful and move on.

When the feelings began affecting my sleep and relationships, I realised I wanted help. I spoke with a qualified professional and described both the life change and the symptoms that had continued.

More than “just getting used to it”

We discussed how long I had felt this way and what was different in my everyday life. The professional did not assume the transition was the only cause or diagnose me from one conversation. We talked about what assessment and support might be appropriate.

I chose one routine I wanted to keep and asked someone I trusted to check in with me. These steps helped me feel less alone, but I continued the conversation with my professional rather than expecting routine alone to solve everything.

Finding an individual next step

I learned that seeking support did not mean I had failed to adapt. My care options needed to fit my health, history and preferences. We planned a follow-up so I could ask questions and talk about what had changed.

What this story can remind us: A major life change and depression symptoms can overlap, but only an individual assessment can help clarify what is happening and what support may fit.

When to get help for persistent low mood

If a change in mood or interest continues, feels distressing or affects your daily life, consider speaking with a qualified professional. If you may be in immediate danger, contact emergency or crisis support now.
