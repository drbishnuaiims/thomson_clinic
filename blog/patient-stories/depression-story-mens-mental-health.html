Looking Beyond “I’m Fine”

An illustrative first-person story about noticing changes in mood, energy and interest—and choosing to talk.

Illustrative composite scenario · Not a real patient testimonial

When friends asked how I was, I said I was fine. In reality, I had become more irritable, stopped enjoying my weekends and struggled to get started in the morning. I did not think of those changes as possible signs of depression; I thought I just needed to be tougher.

A friend noticed I had withdrawn and asked again. That gave me an opening to say I had not felt like myself for a while. I decided to speak with a healthcare professional and describe what had changed rather than minimising it.

Describing what depression can look like for me

I talked about my mood, energy, interest in things and how the changes affected home and work. I learned that depression does not look exactly the same for everyone, and that an assessment considers more than one symptom or stereotype.

We discussed what kinds of follow-up might suit my situation. I also chose to keep one check-in with my friend each week. That connection supported me, while professional care remained part of the conversation.

Asking for help is not weakness

I am learning that I do not need to prove I can handle everything alone. Speaking openly helped me take my symptoms seriously. There is no one-size-fits-all path, and no story can promise how another person will feel.

What this story can remind us: People may describe depression in different words. If you do not feel like yourself, you can talk about what has changed without needing a label first.

Getting help for depression

If low mood or other changes persist or affect daily life, contact a qualified healthcare professional. If you may be in immediate danger or thinking about harming yourself, seek emergency or crisis support now.
