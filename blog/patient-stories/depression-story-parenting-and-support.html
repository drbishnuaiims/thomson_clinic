Depression and parenting

Talking About Depression While Caring for a Young Child

An illustrative first-person story about parenting responsibilities, persistent low mood and asking for compassionate support.

Illustrative composite scenario · Not a real patient testimonial

I loved my child, but I was finding it harder to enjoy the days we spent together. Small tasks felt huge. I was tired, low and often critical of myself for not feeling like the parent I thought I should be.

I kept quiet because I worried that saying “I feel depressed” would make people judge me. Eventually, I told a healthcare professional how long these feelings had been present and how they were affecting my routine. I learned I could ask for help without having every answer first.

Making room to talk honestly

The professional asked about my mood, sleep, support at home and safety. We discussed further assessment and what kinds of care might be appropriate for me. With my permission, I also asked a trusted person to help with a few practical tasks.

Accepting help with meals and childcare gave me a little space to attend appointments and rest. It was practical support, not a cure, and I kept discussing my needs with my care professional.

Support for parents matters

I am learning that parents can care deeply for their children and still need care for themselves. Depression can affect people in different ways. It is okay to tell someone you trust and to ask a professional what support is available.

What this story can remind us: Asking for support is not a measure of whether you are a good parent. Your wellbeing matters, too.

When to seek professional help

If low mood, loss of interest or distress persists or affects daily life, contact a qualified healthcare professional. If there is immediate danger, contact local emergency services or a crisis support service.
