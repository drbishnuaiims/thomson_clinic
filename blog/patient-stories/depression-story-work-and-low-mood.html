Depression and work

When Depression Makes the Workday Feel Heavier

An illustrative first-person story about low mood at work, asking for support and taking a manageable next step.

Illustrative composite scenario · Not a real patient testimonial

I used to finish my workday with energy left for the people and activities I cared about. Over time, even familiar tasks began to feel unusually difficult. I struggled to concentrate, stopped looking forward to things and told colleagues I was simply having a busy month.

When the low mood continued and started affecting my sleep and relationships, I realised I wanted help understanding what was going on. I spoke with a qualified healthcare professional and explained what had changed instead of trying to diagnose myself.

Talking about depression at work

We discussed how I had been feeling and what parts of daily life were affected. I also asked how to think about work while exploring care. My professional helped me consider options that fit my circumstances; I did not have to share personal health details at work before I felt ready.

I chose one practical step: identifying which tasks were most urgent and asking my manager about priorities. That did not treat depression, but it made the next workday feel less overwhelming while I followed up on care.

Seeking support without self-blame

I am learning that struggling at work is not proof that I am lazy or failing. Depression can affect people in different ways, and a professional can help assess symptoms and discuss suitable care. What helps one person may not be right for another.

What this story can remind us: Depression at work may show up as concentration problems, low energy or loss of interest. A conversation with a qualified professional can be a useful first step.

When to seek help for depression

If low mood or other changes persist, cause distress or affect daily life, consider speaking with a qualified healthcare professional. If you may be in immediate danger or are thinking about harming yourself, contact local emergency services or a crisis support service now.
