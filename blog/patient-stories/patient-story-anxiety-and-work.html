Anxiety and work

When Worry Starts Following You to Work

An illustrative story about noticing persistent worry, talking it through and considering a manageable next step.

Illustrative composite scenario · Not a real patient testimonial

I have always cared about doing a good job. Lately, even ordinary tasks make me worry. I reread messages, imagine what could go wrong, and carry work thoughts home with me.

At first, I told myself it was just a busy week. But sleep became less restful, small decisions felt harder, and I wondered if I simply needed to try harder. Eventually, I decided to describe what was happening to a qualified professional.

Finding words for what I was feeling

I talked about when the worry appears, what it gets in the way of, and what I had already tried. I did not need a perfect explanation or a diagnosis before starting the conversation.

Choosing a manageable next step

Together, we talked about options that might suit my circumstances. I chose to notice when the worry rises and to review that pattern at follow-up. It was a small step, not a quick fix or a test I could pass or fail.

I am learning that persistent anxiety is not a personal failure. If worry is interfering with your life too, speaking with a qualified mental health professional can be a reasonable first step.

What this story can remind us: Persistent worry deserves attention. You do not need to wait until you are unable to cope before asking for help.

When should you seek support for anxiety?

Consider speaking with a healthcare professional if worry feels difficult to manage, lasts over time or interferes with sleep, relationships, work or everyday activities. They can help assess your situation and discuss suitable next steps.
