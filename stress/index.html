    How these findings are calculated

    The perceived-stress sub-meter is the mean of Q1–Q6 and reverse-coded Q7–Q8 (Never=0 through Almost always=4), divided by 4 and multiplied by 100. The cumulative meter summarizes normalized response patterns across Q1–Q28 with a neutral baseline while answers are incomplete; Q29–Q30 are kept separate for safety. These are transparent, non-validated descriptions, not the PSS-8/PSS-10 and not clinical categories. Symptom profiles show each response frequency separately; functional impact shows the five response counts and an unthresholded sum out of 20. Duration, trajectory, coping and safety are reported separately. No answers are combined into a clinical severity score.

    Deterministic support rules (not validated cut-offs): urgent assistance takes priority when Q29 is positive and Q30 is Yes or Unsure. Otherwise, prompt professional assessment is recommended if any function response is A lot/Extremely, duration is One to three months/More than three months, trajectory is Worsening, or Q29 is positive. Consider professional support if at least two symptom responses across Q9–Q20 are Several days or more, any symptom response is More than half the days/Nearly every day, any functional response is Moderately or more, or coping confidence is Unsure/Not very confident/Not confident at all. If none applies, self-care and monitoring may be reasonable; this does not rule out a need for help. A low stress index never overrides these rules. This questionnaire does not predict suicide risk.

    This reflection cannot replace care from a qualified professional. Physical symptoms may have psychological, medical or lifestyle-related causes and should not automatically be attributed to stress. If you are concerned, seeking help is reasonable regardless of these responses.
